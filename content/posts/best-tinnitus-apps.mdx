## **<Highlighter>What is the best tinnitus app?</Highlighter>**

There is no single best tinnitus app, because the useful ones solve different problems. For covering up the ringing, **myNoise** gives the most precise control and **Beltone Tinnitus Calmer** is the best free option. For tinnitus that is affecting mood or sleep, **MindEar** and **Oto** are the only apps here with published clinical research behind them. For a free offline sound library with no account, our own **[Tinnitus Help app](/app)** is a reasonable starting point. Most people end up using one masking app and one CBT app together.

## **<Highlighter>First: which kind of tinnitus app do you actually need?</Highlighter>**

Most people download the wrong category of app, decide "apps don't work for tinnitus", and give up. The four categories do genuinely different jobs:

| Category                     | What it does                                                            | Best if...                                                          |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Masking / sound generator** | Plays noise that partially covers the tinnitus                          | The ringing is loudest in quiet rooms and at night                   |
| **Sound therapy / habituation** | Structured daily listening aimed at long-term desensitisation         | You want a programme, not just an on-switch                          |
| **CBT & mental health**      | Retrains the distress and attention response, not the sound             | Tinnitus is causing anxiety, low mood or sleep loss                  |
| **Tracking & insight**       | Logs loudness, mood, sleep and triggers over time                       | You suspect patterns (caffeine, stress, sleep) and want proof        |

If tinnitus is genuinely distressing you, the honest answer is that **the CBT category has the strongest evidence** - better than any masking sound - and the [clinical guidelines](https://www.entnet.org/quality-practice/quality-products/clinical-practice-guidelines/tinnitus/) reflect that. Masking is what makes tonight bearable; CBT is what changes the next six months.

### 1. Tinnitus Help - offline, no account

Our own **Tinnitus Help App** is the one we can describe with the most confidence, so also the one to be most sceptical of us about. It bundles the full article library with the Zen Collection of relief soundscapes - [white noise](/blog/the-power-of-white-noise), [water](/zen/water-sounds), [bird](/zen/bird-sounds), [fire](/zen/fire-sounds) and [nature sounds](/zen/nature-sounds) - plus notifications when new research articles land.

What makes it worth a look: it is **free to download**, there is **no account to create**, and it does not collect a profile on you. What it is not: a therapy programme. There is no CBT course and no clinician behind it. If your tinnitus is causing real distress, one of the CBT apps below will do more for you than we will.

### 2. myNoise - the most controllable masking sounds anywhere

myNoise is the tool serious maskers end up at. Instead of a fixed noise loop, it gives you a **ten-band equaliser over each soundscape**, so you can push energy into exactly the frequency region where your tinnitus sits and leave the rest quiet. For a narrow, high-pitched tone, that precision matters far more than sound library size.

It also has a genuine calibration feature, and the web version is free to try before you install anything. Pricing is refreshingly old-fashioned: the mobile app is a **single one-time purchase** that unlocks the full library for good, with no subscription. Downsides: the interface is dense, and iOS, Android and the website are funded separately, so unlocking one does not unlock the others.

### 3. MindEar - CBT with actual trial data

MindEar delivers **cognitive behavioural therapy** for tinnitus distress through a structured programme with a chatbot coach, built with academic researchers rather than as a pure consumer product, and it has been evaluated in published clinical research rather than only in testimonials - which puts it in a small minority of tinnitus apps. The underlying approach, [CBT for tinnitus](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD012614_cognitive-behavioural-therapy-adults-tinnitus), is the one with the strongest systematic-review support.

Expect it to feel like therapy homework, because that is what it is. It does not try to mask your tinnitus; it targets the anxiety-attention loop that makes tinnitus loud.

### 4. Oto - a broader programme, at subscription prices

Oto combines CBT techniques with sound therapy, relaxation exercises and structured courses, and was founded with ENT clinical input. It is the most "complete package" option here and correspondingly the most expensive. Worth it if you will actually work through a programme; wasted money if you want a sound to fall asleep to.

Note for the many people searching for an **Oto alternative**: MindEar is the closest CBT-focused substitute, and Beltone Tinnitus Calmer is the closest free substitute for its sound therapy half.

### 5. The free hearing-aid manufacturer apps

**Beltone Tinnitus Calmer**, **Widex Zen Tinnitus Management** and **Starkey Relax** are all free to download, and you do not need to own their hearing aids to use them. They are quietly some of the best-value options in the category:

- **Beltone Tinnitus Calmer** - sound therapy plus relaxation and breathing exercises, with a clean interface. Free to use, though the store listing does carry in-app purchases.
- **Widex Zen** - plays **fractal tones**, gentle chime-like sequences that are predictable enough to relax to but varied enough not to grate. Genuinely different from noise, and worth trying if white noise irritates you.
- **Starkey Relax** - simple, well-made masking sounds with per-sound customisation.

If you also have hearing loss, the more important point is this: hearing aids themselves are one of the most effective tinnitus interventions available, and the apps are a fraction of what those devices do.

### 6. Tinnitracks - notched music therapy

Tinnitracks filters your own music to remove a narrow band around your tinnitus frequency, based on the **notched sound therapy** research on lateral inhibition. It requires an audiologist-measured tinnitus frequency, so it is not a casual download, and evidence remains mixed - but for tonal tinnitus with a clearly identifiable pitch it is a legitimate approach. One important caveat if you are outside Germany: it is built around the German healthcare system, where it is listed as a prescribable digital health application and reimbursed by statutory insurers. Elsewhere you are paying out of pocket, and support is geared to German patients. We keep a [notched sound collection](/zen/notched-sounds) if you want to try the concept for free first.

## **<Highlighter>The best tinnitus app for each specific need</Highlighter>**

### Best tinnitus masking app

**myNoise**, for its ten-band equaliser that lets you match the noise to your exact tinnitus pitch. If you want free, **Beltone Tinnitus Calmer** or **Starkey Relax**.

### Best white noise app for tinnitus

Any of the free hearing-aid maker apps (**Beltone**, **Starkey Relax**) or our Tinnitus Help app, which includes a white noise track alongside rain, fan and nature sounds with a sleep timer.

### Best tinnitus sound therapy app

**Widex Zen Tinnitus** for fractal tones if plain noise irritates you, or **Oto** for a structured daily programme rather than a single on-switch.

### Best tinnitus retraining therapy (TRT) app

No app delivers full TRT, which pairs directive counselling with sound therapy under an audiologist. **Oto** and **MindEar** cover the counselling and habituation side; the sound-enrichment half can be any masking app above. For structured TRT, you need a clinician, not an app.

## **<Highlighter>What to look for (and what to avoid)</Highlighter>**

After testing a lot of these, the differences that actually matter:

- **A sleep timer and background playback.** Sounds obvious. Plenty of apps fail at it, and if playback stops when you lock your phone the app is useless at 3am.
- **Frequency control, not just sound count.** One customisable generator beats two hundred fixed loops.
- **Volume that goes low enough.** Many apps have a minimum volume far above what you want for partial masking.
- **Offline playback.** Streaming a masking track all night burns data and fails on bad wifi.
- **No login wall for basic sounds.**

Red flags worth walking away from:

- Any claim to **cure** tinnitus, or a "clinically proven" badge with no citation.
- Apps that require microphone access with no explanation.
- "Tinnitus frequency" tests used as a sales funnel for supplements.
- Subscriptions with no free trial for something you cannot evaluate in five minutes.

## **<Highlighter>What an app genuinely cannot do</Highlighter>**

Being straight about this matters more than another feature list:

An app cannot diagnose you. If your tinnitus is **new and in one ear only**, [pulses in time with your heartbeat](/blog/pulsatile-tinnitus-why-you-hear-your-heartbeat), or arrives with hearing loss, dizziness or ear pain, close the app store and book an appointment - those patterns can have treatable underlying causes.

An app cannot replace a hearing test. If you have hearing loss, treating the hearing loss usually does more for tinnitus than any sound in any app.

And an app cannot do the boring parts: [sleep](/blog/tinnitus-and-sleep), [stress](/blog/tinnitus-and-stress), [caffeine and alcohol](/blog/caffeine-and-tinnitus), [hearing protection at gigs](/blog/tinnitus-in-musicians). Those move the needle more than the choice between two masking apps ever will.

## **<Highlighter>Beyond apps: devices worth knowing about</Highlighter>**

If apps are not enough, the next tier up is hardware:

- **[Lenire](https://www.lenire.com/)** uses [bimodal neuromodulation](/blog/bimodal-neuromodulation-for-tinnitus) - sound plus mild electrical stimulation of the tongue - and holds FDA De Novo authorisation. It is expensive and requires clinical supervision.
- **[Hearing aids with built-in tinnitus programmes](/blog/hearing-aids-and-tinnitus)** from Widex, Signia, ReSound and others combine amplification with sound generation, which is the combination with the best real-world track record.
- **[AirPods Pro's hearing features](/blog/airpods-and-tinnitus)** now include a clinical-grade hearing test and hearing aid mode in supported regions - not a tinnitus treatment, but a genuinely useful and cheap way to find out whether you have hearing loss.

## **<Highlighter>Our recommendation, in one paragraph</Highlighter>**

If you want one thing to try tonight: start with a **masking sound you can reach in the next five minutes** - our app, Beltone Tinnitus Calmer, or myNoise in your browser - and set it just below the level of your tinnitus with a 60-minute sleep timer. If tinnitus is affecting your mood, concentration or sleep on more days than not, the higher-value move is a **CBT-based app** such as MindEar or Oto, or better, [CBT with an actual therapist](/blog/cognitive-behavioral-therapy-and-tinnitus). And if you have not had a hearing test, do that first - it changes which of the above is right for you.
